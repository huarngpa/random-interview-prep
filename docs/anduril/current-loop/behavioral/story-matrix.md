# Story Matrix

Use this page to keep the three stories cohesive and non-overlapping.

## Desired Coverage

| Story | Primary Strength | Backup Strength | Best Round |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Story 1 | product judgment | user empathy + scope control | product / customer focus |
| Story 2 | architecture + systems depth | tradeoffs + execution | project deep dive |
| Story 3 | ambiguity + recovery | leadership + adaptation | either, depending on prompt |

## Selection Rules

We do not want:

- three versions of "I built a backend service"
- three stories with the same tradeoff
- three stories where your role is equally vague

We do want:

- one story where the hard part was finding the right wedge
- one story where the hard part was taming real complexity
- one story where the hard part was recovering from ambiguity, failure, or churn

## Fast Evaluation Rubric

Score each story from `1-5`.

### Product Signal

- was the problem initially unclear?
- did you discover something non-obvious?
- did you shape the first useful version?
- did users or stakeholders change the solution?

### Complexity Signal

- was the system genuinely hard?
- were there real tradeoffs?
- can you explain the design simply?
- can you defend the choice you made?

### Ownership Signal

- can you say exactly what you owned?
- can you separate your work from the team’s work?
- did your decisions change the outcome?

### Failure / Learning Signal

- did something go wrong?
- did you adapt?
- can you explain what you would change now?

## Rotation Strategy

If a prompt is clearly product-focused:

- start from Story 1
- use Story 3 as backup

If a prompt is clearly architecture / complexity-focused:

- start from Story 2
- use Story 3 as backup

If the prompt is broad or behavioral but unclear:

- choose the story with the clearest stakes and ownership

## Probe Checklist

Every story should survive:

- what made this hard?
- what was ambiguous at the start?
- what did you personally own?
- why did you choose that design?
- what alternatives did you reject?
- what went wrong?
- what changed after you learned more?
- what would you do differently now?
